Photographed 23 March 2019 by **JJ Harrison** (https://www.jjharrison.com.au/).

## tern-hero.jpg

1920 x 840, 8 bit greyscale, in use on `index.html`.

Modifications, in order:

1. Cropped to 3840 x 1680 from the 3840 x 2560 Commons thumbnail, taking a horizontal band
   centred on the bird, so the frame is a hero band rather than a full photograph.
2. Converted to monochrome by taking the red channel rather than a luminance average. The water
   is blue, so the red channel renders it darker while the white bird stays bright. This is the
   equivalent of shooting black and white through a red filter, and it is what separates the bird
   from the water without retouching either.
3. Contrast curve applied (sigmoidal, strength 3, midpoint 50 percent). Clipping is negligible:
   0.04 percent of pixels at pure black and 0.04 percent at pure white.
4. Resized to 1920 x 840 and sharpened once to recover the detail lost in the downscale
   (unsharp mask, radius 0, sigma 0.8, amount 0.55, threshold 0.005).
5. Saved as greyscale JPEG, quality 88, no chroma subsampling, metadata stripped and replaced
   with a JPEG comment carrying this attribution and licence.

Nothing was added, removed, cloned, painted or composited in. The background is the photograph's
own water, not a synthetic backdrop, and the bird has not been cut out, stretched or distorted.
The earlier version of this file lifted the levels toward white and feathered the edges of the
frame; that treatment left the bird white on white and the background an uneven haze, so it was
replaced rather than adjusted.

## Why this species

The greater crested tern is the species of Victoria's first detection at Portland (30 July 2026)
and of the South Australian cluster in which the Australian Chief Veterinary Officer reported
evidence of local transmission (29 July 2026). The choice of species is editorial, not decorative.
